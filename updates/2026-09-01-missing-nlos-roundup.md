## 1. Zhou et al., Physical Review Letters 2026

**Yijun Zhou, Wenwen Li, Wei Li, Xin Huang, Chen Dai, Zhong-Pei Xiao, Zheng-Ping Li, Feihu Xu, Jian-Wei Pan, "Non-Line-of-Sight Single-Pixel Imaging Using Polarization Speckle Modulation," Physical Review Letters 136(14), 143801 (2026), DOI 10.1103/kd8v-fykm.**

Contribution: introduces polarization as a new coding degree of freedom for scanning-free NLOS single-pixel imaging. Polarization-dependent speckle patterns generated by a rough relay wall encode the hidden scene; a single-pixel detector recovers the object, with noninvasive calibration using the angular memory effect. This is a strong milestone candidate because it expands NLOS modulation beyond primarily spatial/temporal coding and demonstrates millimeter-scale imaging through a keyhole.

Suggested placement:
- README Latest Additions: high-priority 2026 entry.
- Development timeline: 2026, under active/computational NLOS and scanning-free acquisition.
- Website Paper Explorer: tags `active`, `single-pixel`, `polarization`, `scanning-free`, `computational`.
- Survey: active-acquisition / hardware-and-modulation section, near scan-free, structured illumination, polarization, and compact-system discussions.

Suggested survey sentence:
> Beyond temporal and spatial scanning, Zhou et al. exploited polarization-dependent speckle diversity at a rough relay surface to realize scanning-free single-pixel NLOS imaging, introducing polarization as an additional computational coding degree of freedom for hidden-scene recovery.

## 2. Xiao et al., Optics and Lasers in Engineering 2026

**Zhongpei Xiao, Chen Dai, Ruilin Ye, Jianwei Zeng, Wenwen Li, Feihu Xu, "Non-line-of-sight human pose estimation," Optics and Lasers in Engineering 201, 109658 (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.optlaseng.2026.109658.**

Contribution: task-oriented NLOS inference rather than only reconstruction. The method combines intensity/depth features and a physics-based data-generation pipeline to estimate 3D human pose from transient NLOS measurements, including real measurements and low-SNR / increased relay-distance tests.

Citation-tracing relevance: the paper explicitly positions itself after Velten et al., f-k migration, long-range NLOS and prior HiddenPose-style semantic inference, making it a genuine forward branch from the Core transient-imaging literature rather than a citation-in-passing work.

Suggested placement:
- README Latest Additions and 2026 timeline under detection/recognition/semantic NLOS.
- Website tags `active`, `single-photon`, `human`, `pose`, `semantic`, `learned`.
- Survey: semantic/task-oriented NLOS section near HiddenPose, NLOS-Track/PAC-Net and recent human-orientation/detection works.

Suggested survey sentence:
> Recent work has increasingly bypassed photorealistic reconstruction in favor of task-oriented inference: Xiao et al. combined transient-derived intensity and depth cues with physics-based synthetic training data to estimate hidden human pose under low-SNR and increased stand-off conditions.

## 3. Romanelli et al., Journal of Imaging 2026

**Riccardo Romanelli, Lorenzo Francesco Livi, Francesco V. Pepe, Giacomo Sorelli, Enea Mauri, Milena D'Angelo, Massimiliano Proietti, "Finite-Aperture Limits for Yaw Estimation in Confocal Non-Line-of-Sight Imaging," Journal of Imaging 12(6), 248 (2026), DOI 10.3390/jimaging12060248.**

Contribution: characterizes a measurement-side geometric limit rather than proposing a new reconstruction network. It analyzes how finite relay-wall aperture affects yaw observability using a switch-line visibility criterion and Fisher information, and relates the transition to f-k/backprojection degradation and missing-cone behavior. This is tightly adjacent foundational analysis and belongs in the survey even if it is not a headline algorithmic milestone.

Suggested placement:
- README: theory / limits / geometry subsection rather than headline milestone.
- Website tags `theory`, `confocal`, `finite-aperture`, `missing-cone`, `orientation`.
- Survey: limitations / sampling / missing-cone discussion, especially where relay aperture and reconstruction geometry are explained.

Suggested survey sentence:
> Complementing algorithmic advances, Romanelli et al. quantified how finite relay-wall apertures limit hidden-surface yaw observability, showing through geometric switch-line and Fisher-information analyses that loss of unique reconstruction and loss of useful angular information occur at different stages.
